Start here: is the 'inform the Rent Board' rule a state law or a local law?

Local. The duty to report a rent-controlled unit and hold a Rent Increase License is a San Francisco ordinance — Administrative Code Chapter 37, Section 37.15, added by Ordinance No. 265-20, effective January 18, 2021. It is not a California state statute and not a federal rule.

The 2023 date is the local operative date for small buildings: condominiums and buildings with 1-9 residential units have had to report since March 1, 2023 (buildings with 10+ units began July 1, 2022). The actual 2023 state law was SB 567, which amended the statewide rent cap and just-cause rules and became operative April 1, 2024 — it creates no rent-board reporting duty. S53 · S90 · S88 S73

Next: do I need a Rent Board license as an individual owner?

Yes. A landlord must have a current Rent Increase License on file with the SF Rent Board before an annual or banked rent increase can take effect, and every owner of residential property must report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory first. Owners of buildings with fewer than 10 residential units have had to report since March 1, 2023. No individual-owner, small-portfolio or fewer-than-10-unit exemption was found in the official sources.

Reporting is free and is done in the Rent Board Portal. A notice that goes into effect while the unit is unlicensed may be null and void, and the tenant may be owed a refund. S01 · S07 · S08 S53

A second, separate Rent Board obligation also applies: the annual Rent Board Fee - $59.00 per dwelling unit for 2026-2027, due March 1, with an exemption window closing December 14, 2026. Paying it does not create a license, and a license does not pay it.
