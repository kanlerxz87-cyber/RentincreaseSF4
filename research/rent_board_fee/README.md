# D16-A — Rent Board Fee (Admin Code Ch. 37A) and other landlord obligations that are *not* the Rent Increase License

Session `arena-d16cbfc8` · research date 2026-11-07 (UTC) · every source below is an official City and County of San Francisco page or document, fetched directly on that date with `fetch_page`. Informational research, not legal advice. Nothing below uses the owner's private intake; case facts are the anonymized, owner-supplied ones already published on the site.

## Why this exists

The user's lead question is *what must an individual owner do with the Rent Board before raising rent*. The site already answers the **Housing Inventory report / Rent Increase License** (SF.gov, Admin Code §37.15). Nobody had researched the **second, separate Rent Board obligation: the annual Rent Board Fee** (Admin Code Ch. 37A), nor whether an individual owner of a small building also needs a **City business registration**. A grep of `main` and every `origin/arena/*` branch on 2026-11-07 20:10 UTC found no hit for `37A`, `Rent Board fee` or `business registration`.

## Bottom line (all items verified against the quoted official text below)

1. **The fee is separate from the license.** Paying the fee does not report the unit to the Housing Inventory, and reporting does not pay the fee or request a fee exemption. SF.gov: *"Reporting into the Rent Board Housing Inventory does not satisfy requesting an exemption of the Rent Board Fee."* and *"requesting a Rent Board Fee exemption does not satisfy Rent Board Housing Inventory reporting requirements."*
2. **An individual owner of a rented unit pays it.** *"All residential property owners must pay a per-unit Rent Board fee unless the unit(s) have a current exemption."* The exemptions are narrow (owner-occupied and never rented, government-regulated, tourist-use hotel rooms, certain institutions, nonprofit co-ops, Homeowners' Exemption). A tenant-occupied unit is not an owner-occupied exemption.
3. **Amount for the 2026-2027 tax year: $59.00 per dwelling unit.** Due **March 1** each year (next: March 1, 2027); 5% late penalty added on each of March, April and May (maximum 15%).
4. **The owner may recover at most 50% ($29.50 per unit) from the tenants in occupancy on November 1**, only after the owner has paid in full, never any penalty, and only by (a) deducting it from the next security-deposit interest payment or (b) a direct bill that states the amount, the purpose, and "due and payable within 30 days of the date of the bill". The owner stays liable to the Rent Board either way.
5. **No official page found ties the fee to the validity of a rent increase.** The license turns on Housing Inventory reporting (§37.15(e)). This is a negative finding ("not found on the pages read"), not an official statement that the two are unrelated — kept as `needs review` (FEE-12).
6. **City business registration is generally not required for an individual whose only rental is one residential structure of fewer than four units** — the Treasurer & Tax Collector says such a person *"is not, by reason of that fact alone, required to obtain a business registration certificate"*. The Housing Inventory itself asks for the business registration number only *"if any"* (§37.15(b)(2)(B)). Whether the user's two units are in one structure is owner-supplied/unverified (FEE-14).
