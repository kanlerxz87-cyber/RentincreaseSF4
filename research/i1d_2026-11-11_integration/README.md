## 1. Forefront answer (unchanged, re-verified 2026-11-11)

**Yes — an individual (non-corporate) owner of a covered unit must report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory and have a current Rent Increase License on file before an annual or banked increase takes effect.** No individual-owner, small-portfolio or fewer-than-10-unit exemption was found. Buildings with 1–9 units have reported since March 1, 2023. The legal test is the increase's **effective date** (Rule 13.14), not the service date; obtaining the license before service is the safer practice. "Informing the Rent Board" and "getting the license" are the same process: report via the Rent Board Portal, the license is generated for tenant-occupied units, and it need not be included with the tenant notice. The Rent Board Fee ($59.00/unit, due March 1) is a separate obligation; paying it does not create a license.

Official links: [S01 Learn about rent increases](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco) · [S07 Housing Inventory](https://www.sf.gov/rent-board-housing-inventory) · [S08 Noncompliance notice](https://www.sf.gov/notice-of-owner-noncompliance-with-housing-inventory) · [S53 Sec. 37.15](https://www.sf.gov/information--sec-3715-reporting-obligations-licensing) · [S73 Rule 13.14](https://www.sf.gov/reports--december-2022--rent-board-rules-and-regulations-part-xiii-reporting-obligations-under-rent)
