Is the Rent Board Fee the same thing as the Rent Increase License?
Q-15
No. They are separate obligations. The license comes from Housing Inventory reporting and there is no charge for that reporting; the fee is a separate per-unit annual payment (2026-2027: $59.00 per dwelling unit, due March 1, with a 5% monthly late penalty to a 15% maximum). SF.gov states expressly that requesting a fee exemption does not satisfy Housing Inventory reporting.rule checked
The Rent Board Fee (S56)The Rent Board Housing Inventory (S07)Sec. 37.15 Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S53)
Paying the fee is not a substitute for the license; see flag F33.
Can part of the Rent Board Fee be charged to the tenants?
Q-16
Yes, if the fee is paid in full: 50% of the fee, i.e. $29.50 per dwelling unit for 2026-2027, may be recovered from tenants in occupancy as of November 1 of the applicable tax year. Penalties may not be recovered.rule checked

Are these two units exempt from AB 1482 because the owner lives in one?
Q-19
Only if the owner occupied one unit as a principal residence when the tenancy began and continues in occupancy, and neither unit is an ADU/JADU. Does an in-law unit or a second structure change whether these units are covered?
Q-20
It can. SF.gov states that a dwelling with a legal or illegal in-law unit is a two-unit building and is not exempt from the Rent Ordinance unless both units are rented together as a single tenancy, and that a single-family dwelling is not exempt if another residential structure is on the same lot. Two rented units is consistent with Rent Ordinance coverage, but the official lot and building record was not supplied.rule checked
