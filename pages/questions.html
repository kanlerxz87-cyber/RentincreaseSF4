What are reasonable/normal rental increases under local, state and federal law?
Q-01		Local 1.6% for covered units; state-only AB 1482 8.8% does not replace SF limits; no official general federal private-rent cap found. HUD FMR is not a cap.verifiedS01 S27 S43 S44Federal cap blank and flagged.
How do big real estate corporations handle increases?
Q-02		Public REITs disclose lease-over-lease rates in SEC filings: IRT Q4 2025 renewal 2.9%, new -3.7%, blended 0.8%; Invitation Homes 2025 renewal 4.6%, new -0.8%.verifiedS48 S49Portfolio-level, not SF-specific; no generalization.
How do individual SF property owners handle increases?
Q-03		Official rules require the annual allowable increase, license, 12-month timing and notice; June 2024 Rent Board workload shows 23 landlord petitions/154 units.verifiedS01 S07 S29Narrow formal-petition evidence only.
What is the correct tenant-notice method?
Q-04		Written 30-day notice; 90-day if >10% alone/combined in same year; +5 days if mailed; dollar amount, percentage, effective date; banked portion if any; license first; 827 delivery rules.verifiedS01 S05 S35 S07Conservative checklist capable of lawyer review.

Do individual owners (not corporations) need to apply for the Rent Board license and inform the Rent Board before notifying tenants?
Q-11		Yes. A current Rent Increase License must be on file before an annual or banked increase takes effect, and all owners must report into the Housing Inventory first; no individual-owner or small-building exemption was found (buildings under 10 units report from March 1, 2023).verifiedS01 S07 S08 S53See the Step-by-Step License page.
Is there a separate form to tell the Rent Board about each individual increase?
Q-12		The official pages describe only the annual Housing Inventory report as the route to the license; no separate filing of the increase notice is described, and the license need not be included with the notice.verifiedS07 S53 S01Absence of a stated requirement; counselor confirmation suggested.
What happens if a notice goes out without a current license?
Q-13		The increase may be null and void, the tenant may be entitled to a refund and may petition the Rent Board, which can schedule an Administrative Law Judge hearing.verifiedS08 S53 S25 S26A tenant summary petition also prompts a letter to the landlord.
Does the added roommate allow a market-rate reset?
Q-14		No, not by itself: extra rent for an additional occupant is unlawful, and a 6.14/Costa-Hawkins increase requires specific facts plus a timely written 6.14 notice. Those facts are unverified here.verifiedS01 S18 S16 S37No reset recommended; see flag F28.
Is the Rent Board Fee the same thing as the Rent Increase License?
Q-15		No. They are separate obligations. The license comes from Housing Inventory reporting and there is no charge for that reporting; the fee is a separate per-unit annual payment (2026-2027: $59.00 per dwelling unit, due March 1, with a 5% monthly late penalty to a 15% maximum). SF.gov states expressly that requesting a fee exemption does not satisfy Housing Inventory reporting.verifiedS56 S07 S53Paying the fee is not a substitute for the license; see flag F33.
Can part of the Rent Board Fee be charged to the tenants?
Q-16		Yes, if the fee is paid in full: 50% of the fee, i.e. $29.50 per dwelling unit for 2026-2027, may be recovered from tenants in occupancy as of November 1 of the applicable tax year. Penalties may not be recovered.verifiedS56This is a fee recovery, not a rent increase; keep it separately identified.
How long do I have to report a Housing Inventory change, and what if I already served a notice?
Q-17		Unit information is updated annually by March 1, and a change in the owner or manager name or business contact information must be updated within 30 days. If a notice already went out with no license on file, report first, then re-serve the notice so the effective date falls after the license is on file: a notice is unenforceable for an effective date on which no license was on file, and an unlicensed increase may be null and void.verifiedS53 S07 S08Whether any notice has already been served here is unverified.
Can an increase during a repair dispute or after a complaint be retaliatory?
Q-18		Yes, that is a separate state-law risk. If the tenant is not in default and has asked for repairs, given a tenantability notice, or complained to an agency, the owner may not increase the rent within 180 days of that act; retaliation for tenant organizing or for exercising lawful rights is separately barred. Remedies include actual damages, punitive damages of $100-$2,000 per retaliatory act, and attorney's fees.verifiedS57 S22 S42Timing versus any open habitability complaint is unverified; this is not a legal opinion.
Are these two units exempt from AB 1482 because the owner lives in one?
Q-19		Only if the owner occupied one unit as a principal residence when the tenancy began and continues in occupancy, and neither unit is an ADU/JADU. That fact was not supplied, so no exemption is asserted. Independently, AB 1482's rent cap does not apply where the unit is already under the SF Rent Ordinance limits, which are stricter.verifiedS58 S60 S27Conditional; see flags F34 and F35.
Does an in-law unit or a second structure change whether these units are covered?
Q-20		It can. SF.gov states that a single-family dwelling with a legal in-law unit is a two-unit building and is not exempt, that with an illegal in-law unit it is also a two-unit building and is not exempt unless both units are rented together as a single tenancy, and that a single-family dwelling is not exempt if another residential structure is on the same lot. Two rented units is consistent with Rent Ordinance coverage, but the official lot and building record was not supplied.verifiedS60 S59 S06Verify the lot in the Property Information Map; see flags F35 and F36.
Do the tenants have to keep accepting increases, or can they challenge each one?
Q-21		They can challenge. A tenant may file an unlawful-rent-increase petition with no filing fee and no time limit, and the matter can go to an Administrative Law Judge. Tenant-side free legal help is documented for eviction proceedings (Eviction Defense Collaborative line and Tenant Right to Counsel), not as a general pre-approval service for rent increases.verifiedS25 S61 S30No official source offers approval of an increase before it is served.
