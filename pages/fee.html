Fee basics - a separate obligation from the Rent Increase License

ItemValue / findingStatusOfficial source(s)Notes / flag
Who must pay the Rent Board Fee
FEE-01		An owner of a residential dwelling unit or guest unit must pay a Rent Board Fee each year unless there is a current exemption on file with the Rent Board or a Homeowners' Exemption on file with the Assessor-Recorder. No individual-owner or small-building exception is stated.verifiedS56This is a fee obligation, not a licence application.
What the fee is charged on
FEE-02		All residential property owners must pay a per-unit fee unless the unit has a current exemption. This includes single-family homes and condominiums, units built after 1979, vacant units, and units occupied by friends or family members.verifiedS56Rent-control coverage is not the test for the fee; exemption is.
Amount for the 2026-2027 tax year
FEE-03		$59.00 per dwelling unit; $29.50 per guest unit (single room occupancy or SRO/residential hotel room). The 50% tenant share is $29.50 per dwelling unit or $14.75 per guest unit.verifiedS56 S64Form 573 confirms $59.00 per unit for 7/21-6/22 through 7/25-6/26 (flag F40 on the form's title metadata).
Paying the fee does not make you licensed
FEE-04		SF.gov states that requesting a Rent Board Fee exemption does not satisfy Housing Inventory reporting requirements, and the 2027 informational notice states the reverse: Housing Inventory reporting does not satisfy a fee exemption. The licence arises only from the Housing Inventory report.verifiedS56 S65 S53Two separate obligations; no official page found conditions the licence on fee payment (flag F41).
How and where to pay
FEE-05		Preferred: credit card or electronic check (ACH) online through the Rent Board Portal. In person: Office of the Treasurer and Tax Collector, City Hall Room 140. By mail: San Francisco Rent Board, PO Box 7429, San Francisco, CA 94120-7429. No payments are accepted at the Rent Board office.verifiedS56A dishonoured check or eCheck adds a $50.00 payment failure fee.
Statutory scope of Chapter 37A
FEE-06		Chapter 37A applies to all residential units in San Francisco, including units exempt from the Chapter 37 rent-increase limitations under the Costa-Hawkins Act or Section 37.3(d), with listed exclusions (nonprofit co-ops, licensed care facilities and dormitories, government-regulated rents such as Section 8, tourist-use guest rooms, and units with a current homeowners' exemption).verifiedS63Ordinance text, not just the SF.gov summary.

Deadlines, penalties and the 2026-27 compliance calendar

ItemValue / findingStatusOfficial source(s)Notes / flag
Due date and late penalties
FEE-07		The fee is due March 1 each year. A 5% late penalty is added if unpaid by March 1, a further 5% if unpaid by April 1, and a further 5% if unpaid by May 1 - a maximum penalty of 15%.verifiedS56 S63SF.gov and Section 37A.4 agree on the March/April/May sequence.
Delinquency and referral to the Bureau of Delinquent Revenue
FEE-08		SF.gov states that fees not paid in full by June 1 will be referred to the Bureau of Delinquent Revenue with additional costs added to the principal charges; its timeline instead says a Notice of Delinquent Payment is mailed in July, after which the owner has 30 days before referral. Section 37A.4 says the matter is referred if the fee remains outstanding as of June 1.verifiedS56 S63The official page is internally inconsistent - see flag F38. Manual confirmation with 311 is advised.
Payment failure fee
FEE-09		If a check or eCheck payment is not honoured by the bank, the payment is null and void and a $50.00 payment failure fee is charged in addition to any late payment penalties.verifiedS56Not recoverable from a tenant.
Exemption request window and annual refiling
FEE-10		Requests for exemption are submitted through the Rent Board Portal between October and early December, and an exemption request must be submitted every year. For the 2027 fee the deadline is Monday, December 14, 2026; units without a valid approved exemption are billed for the fee due March 1, 2027.verifiedS56 S65Homeowners' Exemption holders do not refile yearly with the Rent Board.
2026-27 calendar as published
FEE-11		October: informational notices mailed, exemption window opens. December 14, 2026: exemption request deadline. January 2027: annual notices (fee invoices) mailed. March 1, 2027: fee due with no penalty; first 5% penalty from March 2. April 1 and May 1: second and third 5% penalties. May 31, 11:59 pm: last day to pay with all penalties. June 1: outstanding bills delinquent. July: notice of delinquent payment mailed.verifiedS56 S65Housing Inventory reporting is also due March 1, 2027 - see the Step-by-Step License page.

Exemptions are narrow - a rented unit is not exempt

ItemValue / findingStatusOfficial source(s)Notes / flag
Owner-occupied exemption
FEE-13		A single-family home, condominium, co-op or apartment unit occupied by an owner of record and not rented at any time may qualify. Vacant units, and units occupied solely by children or close relatives who are not on title as owner, do not qualify. Section 37A.1(f) requires the owner to file an affidavit with the Rent Board.verifiedS56 S63Both units here are rented, so this exemption would not apply on the owner-supplied facts.
Other exemption categories
FEE-14		Government-controlled or regulated rents (for example Section 8, with documentation), hotel guest rooms designed for tourist use under the Residential Hotel Ordinance, housing in hospitals, monasteries, extended-care facilities and licensed homes for the elderly, dormitories owned by educational institutions, and nonprofit cooperatives owned, occupied and controlled by a majority of residents.verifiedS56 S63Documentation is required for each category.
Homeowners' Exemption route
FEE-15		An owner whose unit is the primary residence and who already has a Homeowners' Exemption through the Assessor-Recorder should not be assessed the Rent Board fee and may email proof to the Rent Board. The Homeowners' Exemption applies only to the current fee cycle and cannot be applied retroactively unless it appeared on earlier property tax statements.verifiedS56The Rent Board does not process Homeowners' Exemption applications.

Recovering 50% of the fee from tenants - not a rent increase

ItemValue / findingStatusOfficial source(s)Notes / flag
Maximum recovery and who can be charged
FEE-16		An owner who has paid the fee in full may recover up to 50% of the annual fee (exclusive of any penalties) from the tenant(s) in occupancy of each unit on November 1 of the applicable tax year. For 2026-2027 that is $29.50 per dwelling unit.verifiedS56 S62 S63Only tenants in occupancy on or before November 1 can be billed.
How the recovery may be made
FEE-17		By deducting the amount from the next security-deposit interest payment owed to the tenant, or by billing the tenant directly - but direct billing is allowed only where there is no or insufficient security deposit held, or where the owner already pays the deposit interest annually. The bill must state the amount for that unit, that the purpose is to fund the Rent Board and related administrative costs, and that the amount is due and payable within 30 days of the bill.verifiedS62 S63Do not net it into the rent figure on a rent-increase notice.
Banking an unrecovered fee
FEE-18		A fee not recovered in the year the owner was first entitled to it may be banked and collected from that tenant in a future year. Only fees that became due after July 25, 1999 may be banked, and the billing statement must show the fee owed for each year and any interest due for each year.verifiedS62 S63Banking is by deposit-interest deduction except where no or insufficient interest exists.
If the tenant does not pay
FEE-19		A tenant's failure to pay the Rent Board fee is not a just cause for eviction; the landlord must go to Small Claims Court to collect an unpaid fee.verifiedS62The owner remains liable to the Rent Board either way (Section 37A.6(d)).
Security-deposit interest rate used for a deduction
FEE-20		The interest rate owed on deposits for March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027 is 4.2%. Interest is paid annually on the tenant's annual due date for deposits held over one year and may not be compounded. AB 12 caps a deposit at one month's rent (two months for a qualifying small natural-person owner), for deposits collected on or after July 1, 2024.verifiedS67This is the amount a fee recovery is deducted from.

Recovery is not part of the rent increase
FEE-22		The fee recovery is a separate statutory billing mechanism tied to the security-deposit interest cycle; it is not an annual or banked rent increase, does not change the base rent, and is not covered by the Rent Increase License precondition for annual/banked increases.analysisS62 S63 S01Keep the two documents and the two amounts visibly separate.

Other registration question - City business registration

ItemValue / findingStatusOfficial source(s)Notes / flag
Does an individual small owner need a City business registration certificate?
FEE-23		A person receiving rental income from one residential structure consisting of fewer than four units, or from one residential condominium, is not - by reason of that fact alone - required to obtain a business registration certificate. Other business activity or payroll expense may require one. A lessor is treated as a separate person for each individual building, the Tax Collector decides what a separate building is and how many units it has, and a return must be filed unless the lessor leases fewer than 4 units in any individual building.verifiedS66Rule only. For this owner it depends on facts not supplied - see the next row.
What the Housing Inventory asks for
FEE-24		Section 37.15(b)(2)(B) requires the business registration number for the unit, if any - so reporting does not assume that every owner has one.verifiedS53Absence of a business registration number is not, by itself, a reporting failure.
