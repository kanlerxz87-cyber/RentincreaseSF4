Does any state law reviewed require telling a rent board about a rent increase?
ST-19		No. Read in full on 2026-11-13, Civil Code Sec. 1946.2 (just cause), Sec. 1947.12 (cap), Sec. 827 (notice), Sec. 1942.5 (retaliation) and Sec. 1947.7 (substantial compliance with a local rent program) contain no requirement that an owner notify, file with, or obtain permission from a rent board or any other agency before imposing a rent increase. Sec. 1947.12(e) requires only notice to the tenant under Sec. 827verifiedS90 S36 S35 S57 S91The Rent Board reporting/licence duty is local (Sec. 37.15). Scope limit: this is a review of the named sections plus the SF pages, not an exhaustive search of the California Codes - see flag F55.
State backstop if a local filing was late: Civil Code Sec. 1947.7
ST-20		Intent is 'to limit the imposition of penalties and sanctions against an owner of residential rental units where that person has attempted in good faith to fully comply with the regulatory processes' of a local rent program. An owner in substantial compliance with a rent-control ordinance 'which requires the registration of rents' 'shall not be assessed a penalty or any other sanction for noncompliance', with restitution to the tenant or recovery of registration/filing fees as the exclusive remedies; and 'all annual rent adjustments which may have been denied during the period of the owner's noncompliance shall be restored prospectively once the owner is in compliance'verifiedS91 S53 S89Do not rely on it yet: SF's ordinance states the Housing Inventory is NOT a rental registry within the meaning of Sections 1947.7-1947.8, so its application to the SF licence scheme is unsettled (Q-24, flag F56). Locally, Rule 13.14 separately preserves the right to re-impose an increase voided for lack of a licence.
