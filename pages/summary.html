ItemValue / findingStatusOfficial source(s)Notes / flag
Does an individual (non-corporate) owner still need the license?
LIC-01		Yes - SF.gov states 'a landlord must get a rent increase license before imposing annual allowable and/or banked rent increases'; no individual-owner exception is stated on the official pages re-fetched 2026-11-07verifiedS01 S07Ownership form (individual vs corporation) is not listed as an exemption. Do not rely on being a small/individual owner.
Small buildings (1-9 units) timing
LIC-02		Requirement effective July 1, 2022; March 1, 2023 for condominiums and buildings with 1-9 residential unitsverifiedS01 S07A small individual-owned building is therefore covered now (2026).
Step 1 - Report to the Rent Board (Housing Inventory)
LIC-03		All owners of residential property are required to report through the Rent Board Portal; reporting is free, online 24/7, due March 1 every yearverifiedS07This reporting is how you 'inform the Rent Board'. Information submitted is public; submit under penalty of perjury.
Step 2 - License is generated
LIC-04		Property owners who report a unit as tenant-occupied receive a rent increase license; it is automatically generated for non-owner-occupied unitsverifiedS07The license does not need to be included with the rent increase notice to tenants.
Step 3 - License must be current BEFORE the increase takes effect
LIC-05		A landlord must have a current rent increase license on file with the Rent Board before any annual or banked rent increase can go into effect; a notice is not enforceable without a license on the effective dateverifiedS07 S01Order matters: Housing Inventory report -> license on file -> then serve tenant notice -> effective date. See flag F25 on consequence wording.
Late reporting
LIC-06		You can still submit and receive a license after the March 1 deadline; the Rent Board does not assess monetary penalties for late filing - but the increase notice is not enforceable without a license on the effective dateverifiedS07Late filing does not cure a notice whose effective date passed without a license; get legal advice on any already-served notice.
Step 4 - Only then notify tenants
LIC-07		30-day written notice (90-day if >10% alone/combined in same year; +5 days if mailed) stating dollar amount, percentage and effective date, using the rate in effect on the increase dateverifiedS01 S05 S35License first, tenant notice second. License alone does not satisfy state/local notice law; you must still comply with all other rent increase laws.
Can tenants check?
LIC-08		Tenants can search the Rent Board Portal to see if their landlord is up to date with their rent increase license; if you miss the deadline the Rent Board may notify tenants you are not in complianceverifiedS07 S08Assume tenants and free tenant-side legal services can and will check this before paying an increase.
