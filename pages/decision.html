Gate 1 - Coverage: the unit is actually subject to the SF Rent Ordinance
DR-G1		Both units are two rented units in a 1940 building, which SF.gov describes as the paradigm covered case (many units built on or before June 13, 1979 have rent control). Owner-supplied facts; the official record is unchecked.analysisS06 S32 S59If the unit is exempt, the 1.6% limit, the license and the Rent Board notice rules do not apply at all - a different decision entirely.
Gate 2 - Standing: the owner is entitled to impose an increase at all
DR-G2		A landlord must have a current Rent Increase License on file before an annual or banked increase takes effect; the license follows the unit's rent-controlled status, not the owner's corporate form.verifiedS01 S07 S53 S73Rule 13.14 makes an unlicensed increase null and void as of its effective date - so this gate can void a fully correct notice.
Gate 3 - Amount: the correct base rent, rate and elapsed time
DR-G3		The rate is 1.6% for 3/1/26-2/28/27 and an increase may be imposed once every 12 months on the anniversary date, on base rent only, not rounded up.verifiedS01 S02 S05A rate that is right but applied to the wrong base rent or too early is still an unlawful increase.
Gate 4 - Process: the notice and delivery meet both local and state rules
DR-G4		30-day written notice (90-day if more than 10% alone or combined in the same year), plus 5 days if mailed within California; the notice must state the dollar amount, the percentage and the effective date, using the rate in effect on the increase date.verifiedS01 S35 S78A correct amount in a defective notice can still be unenforceable. Civil Code 827 content and service rules are cumulative with the local rules.
Gate 5 - Fair treatment: the choice between the two units is defensible
DR-G5		Federal and state fair-housing law reach discriminatory effect without intent; a substantial legitimate non-discriminatory interest and the absence of a less discriminatory alternative are the test. No official source requires raising both units.verifiedS42 S38 S41This is the gate that answers the one-unit-or-both question, and it is decided by the reason for the choice, not the size of the increase.

DR-02		SF.gov lists partial exemptions for units built after June 13, 1979, most single-family homes and condominiums, substantial rehabilitation, and government-regulated rents; a legal or illegal in-law unit does not by itself create an exemption and can keep a two-unit building covered.verifiedS06 S59 S60Exemption status is unverified for this address. DR-03		Civil Code 1947.12(d)(6) exempts a two-unit structure only where the owner occupied one unit as a principal residence at the beginning of the tenancy and continues in occupancy, and neither unit is an ADU or JADU. Does any official source require raising both units together?
DR-17		No. No official source located requires an owner to raise rent on every unit, and no official source prohibits raising one.verifiedS42 S41 S38This is a negative finding. What legal standard would a fair-housing inquiry actually apply?
DR-18		Disparate-impact liability can exist without intent; a policy is defensible where it serves a substantial legitimate non-discriminatory interest and there is no less discriminatory alternative. California FEHA separately reaches source of income, retaliation, perception and association.verifiedS42 S38 S39Practical consequence: select units by a rule that does not reference the tenant (anniversary date, licence status, coverage), and write the rule down before applying it.
