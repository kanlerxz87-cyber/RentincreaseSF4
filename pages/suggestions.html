Site Suggestions - Review Before Implementing

Dedicated decision board: ways to improve this site so a decision can be made from it, grouped by purpose, each with impact, effort and risk. Nothing on this page has been implemented; pick a path first.

Informational compliance research, not legal advice. No attorney has approved a proposed increase and nothing here guarantees that a complaint, inquiry or lawsuit will not occur. Any increase is framed only as a conservative compliance checklist capable of tenant-lawyer review.

How to use this board

What this page is (and is not)
SG-USE-01		A proposal board, not a change log. Each row is an option for improving the site's decision value. The underlying research rows, rates, dates and dollar amounts are fixed by official sources and are not offered for change here.analysisS68Status 'analysis' = a reasoned proposal, not an official requirement. Read with REQ-26, which records that this page exists and that no suggestion on it is implemented (flag F53).
How to choose a path
SG-USE-02		Work the sections in order: A (decide-first) then B (missing information) then C/D/E (verification, privacy, presentation). Mark each row accept, defer or reject in the decisions table at the bottom, then pick one of the three pre-built sequences (SG-51 to SG-53). Blank decision cells mean 'not chosen', and nothing should be implemented before the cells are filled.analysisS68Effort labels: low = copy or one table; medium = new page section plus validator checks; high = new source research or an owner-only action. Risk if skipped is what a reviewer would find missing.
What is deliberately not suggested
SG-USE-03		No suggestion may infer a value, compute a banked total from missing records, assert eligibility, or imply attorney approval or immunity from challenge. Those would breach the project's own no-inference rule and the disclaimer on every page.analysisS68 S01If a suggestion needs a fact the owner has not supplied, it is written as 'go and find that fact' (section B) rather than as a number.

A. Decide-first suggestions - turn the research into an answer

SuggestionWhat to change and why it improves decision-makingStatusOfficial source(s) / basisEffort - risk - dependencies
Per-unit one-page decision sheet
SG-01		Change: add a page section per unit that stacks, in order, the eight facts a notice depends on - coverage, Housing Inventory confirmation, licence on the intended effective date, base rent, anniversary date, rate in effect on that date, computed amount, notice served on / by whom - each with a blank owner-input cell and its official link. Why: today those facts sit on six different pages, so the pre-notice gate has to be assembled by hand, which is how a licence or an anniversary date gets missed. Effect: the owner and any reviewer can see in one screen whether a specific notice is ready.analysisS01 S07 S02 S35Effort: medium. Risk if skipped: a notice is served while a gate is still blank. Depends on: SG-11 (the records). Reuses the B87 worksheet gates rather than duplicating them (pages/worksheet.html).
Hard-stop banner while a gate is blank
SG-02		Change: promote a single red 'Do not serve a notice yet' banner to the landing page and the case pages, listing which specific cells are still blank (licence status, increase history, occupant status). Why: the site already reports blanks honestly, but they are visible only row by row; a decision-maker skimming the dashboard sees conclusions, not gaps. Effect: the site refuses to look more ready than it is.analysisS07 S08 S68Effort: low. Risk if skipped: false confidence from the dashboard cards. Depends on: nothing - the blank counts are already computed at build time.
Reverse-planned date calendar per unit
SG-03		Change: from a target effective date, work backwards and show the three binding dates: the last date the 1.6% year applies (February 28, 2027), the notice service date (30 days, plus 5 if mailed), and the Housing Inventory/licence check date. Why: the rate year turns on March 1 and both units' tenancies run from the 1st of a month, so the effective date is the single highest-leverage choice in this case. Effect: converts '1.6%' into 'serve by this date or use the next published rate'.analysisS02 S01 S78 S35Effort: medium. Risk if skipped: a notice priced on 1.6% lands on or after March 1, 2027, when the 2027-28 rate is not yet published (F27). Depends on: the owner's target date.
Rate-boundary warning on every calculation row
SG-04		Change: add a standing caution to the Calculations page: any increase effective on or after March 1, 2027 must use the rate published for that year, which does not exist yet; the published 1.6% ends February 28, 2027. Why: four of the current calculation rows are illustrative at today's rate, and a reader may carry that number into a notice that takes effect after the boundary. Effect: prevents the most likely arithmetic error in this case.analysisS02 S01Effort: low. Risk if skipped: a notice quotes a rate that will not apply. Depends on: nothing new; F27 already records the unpublished rate.
Side-by-side scenario grid (no new numbers)
SG-05		Change: a 3x3 grid - Unit 1 / Unit 2 / both, crossed with annual-only / annual-plus-banked / defer to next rate year - where each cell states what is known, what is blank, and which official rule governs; banked cells stay explicitly blank because the increase history was never supplied. Why: the real choice is not the percentage but which unit and which mechanism, and the grid makes the blanks the headline. Effect: the owner can see that 'raise both, annual only' is the only scenario currently supported by the record.analysisS05 S54 S01Effort: medium. Risk if skipped: a banked or reset theory is adopted without the records that would support it. Depends on: SG-11, SG-12.
Cost-to-reason map for the 'reasonable reasons' question
SG-06		Change: turn the Reasonable Reasons page into a two-column map - each cost the owner expects to face (property taxes, insurance, repairs, the Rent Board fee, maintenance) against the only mechanism the law allows for recovering it (annual allowable, none, O&M petition, capital improvement petition, passthrough, fee recovery) with the cap and the petition-before-notice rule on each row. Why: 'reasonable' in the brief is a legal question about mechanism, not about fairness; this table is what a tenant-side reviewer will check first. Effect: stops a cost list from being used as if it raised the 1.6% cap.analysisS09 S11 S12 S56 S62Effort: medium. Risk if skipped: a 'costs' justification invites an unlawful-increase petition. Depends on: nothing.

B. Missing-information suggestions - what to go and find

SuggestionWhat to change and why it improves decision-makingStatusOfficial source(s) / basisEffort - risk - dependencies
Collect the four primary records and stop researching until they are in hand
SG-11		Change: obtain and file (1) the Rent Board Portal confirmation and licence record for each unit for each year, (2) each current lease and any addendum or renewal, (3) every rent-increase notice ever served with its proof of service, and (4) the deposit-interest/fee statements. Why: three of the site's most consequential blanks - licence status, anniversary dates, banked amounts - are answered by documents the owner already holds or can download in minutes. Effect: converts the largest analysis page on this site from inference to record.analysisS07 S55 S26 S67Effort: low for the owner, high for the site (it fills ~20 blank cells). Risk if skipped: every downstream number stays illustrative. Depends on: nothing.
Pull the official rent history from the Rent Board
SG-12		Change: request the Rent Board's record of reported rents for both units (the same record a tenant can use to challenge an increase) and compare it with the owner's own file. Why: where the owner's memory and the Board's file disagree, the Board's file is what a petition hearing starts from. Effect: fixes the base rent and the increase dates from an official source instead of owner-supplied intake.analysisS25 S26 S07Effort: medium. Risk if skipped: the base rent used for the 1.6% is unverified (T03-F06). Depends on: nothing.
Resolve the April 1, 2026 occupant question before touching Unit 1
SG-13		Change: establish from the lease and the rent roll whether the person added on April 1, 2026 became a co-tenant on the original tenancy or an occupant/subtenant, and whether any written Section 6.14 notice was ever served. Why: that single fact decides whether any vacancy-based or market-reset theory exists at all, and the site currently refuses to guess. Effect: either closes the reset question permanently or identifies the petition route and its proof burden.analysisS15 S16 S17 S18Effort: low for the owner. Risk if skipped: an unlawful increase is served on a mistaken decontrol theory. Depends on: nothing.
Confirm coverage and lot configuration officially
SG-14		Change: capture the Property Information Map / Assessor record for the address: year built, unit count, whether the two units are in one structure or a main house plus an in-law or second structure. Why: coverage drives everything else, and the 1940 year is owner-supplied. Effect: replaces the 'likely covered' caveat with a dated official record, and settles the exemption analysis (ST-16, LOC-23).analysisS06 S32 S60Effort: low. Risk if skipped: the whole analysis rests on an unconfirmed building year. Depends on: nothing.
Answer the owner-occupancy question for the state two-unit exemption
SG-15		Change: record whether the owner occupied one unit of the building as a principal residence at the beginning of each tenancy and continues to do so, and whether either unit is an ADU/JADU. Why: it is the only state exemption that could plausibly apply to a two-unit building, and both the exemption test and its conditions are already on the site but unanswered. Effect: settles whether Civil Code sections even enter the analysis for these units.analysisS58 S90 S27Effort: low for the owner. Risk if skipped: an exemption is assumed or ignored by default. Depends on: nothing.
Build the 180-day retaliation and repair log now, not later
SG-16		Change: a dated list of every tenant repair request, habitability notice, agency complaint, inspection or proceeding in the 180 days before any intended notice, plus the owner's response dates. Why: Civil Code 1942.5 turns on timing and knowledge, and the record is easy to assemble today and hard to reconstruct after a dispute. Effect: turns the retaliation section from a warning into an evidence file.analysisS57 S82 S22Effort: low. Risk if skipped: an increase landing inside the window looks reactive. Depends on: nothing.

Risk if skipped: over-reading of owner-supplied rows. Depends on: nothing.

Effect: makes the site auditable in one place at a glance.analysisS68Effort: medium. Risk if skipped: silent drift between visits. Depends on: the build already writing data/validation_report.json.
