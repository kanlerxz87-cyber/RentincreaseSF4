Must an individual property owner (not a corporation) get a Rent Increase License before raising rent?
PRC-01
Yes. A landlord must have a current Rent Increase License on file before any annual or banked increase can take effect. The requirement follows the unit's rent-controlled status, not the owner's size or corporate form.rule checked
Learn about rent increases in San Francisco (S01)The Rent Board Housing Inventory (S07)Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)Sec. 37.15 Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S53)
No owner-type, individual-owner or small-portfolio exemption appears on the official pages.
What does the Rent Ordinance itself say?
PRC-02
Section 37.3(a)(1): a landlord who has reported the required information under Section 37.15 shall have a license to impose an annual increase not exceeding 60% of the published CPI increase, never more than 7%. Section 37.15(e): a substantially compliant owner receives the license; otherwise it is suspended and the tenant is not obligated to pay the increased rent for the months of the suspension.rule checked
Sec. 37.3 Rent Limitations (S21)Sec. 37.15 Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S53)
The license condition is written into the ordinance, not only the SF.gov summaries.
Is there a small-owner or fewer-than-10-unit exemption?
PRC-03
No exemption found. Condominiums and units in buildings with fewer than 10 residential units have been required to report since March 1, 2023, with updates every March 1.rule checked
The Rent Board Housing Inventory (S07)Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)Sec. 37.15 Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S53)
Applies to this two-unit building if the units are rent-controlled.
Must the owner report into the Housing Inventory first?
PRC-04
Yes. All owners of residential property are required to report; a rent increase license is generated for units reported as tenant-occupied.rule checked
The Rent Board Housing Inventory (S07)Sec. 37.15 Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S53)
Free; annual deadline March 1; no monetary penalty for late filing.
Does informing the Rent Board mean filing each increase notice?
PRC-05
The official pages describe the license as arising from the annual Housing Inventory report. No separate filing of an individual increase notice is described, and the license does not need to be included with the notice.analysis
The Rent Board Housing Inventory (S07)Sec. 37.15 Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S53)Learn about rent increases in San Francisco (S01)
Absence of a stated separate filing requirement is not an official confirmation; ordinary annual/banked increases differ from petition-based increases.
What happens if a notice goes into effect without a current license?
PRC-06
The notice may be null and void (possibly for multiple years) or unenforceable, depending on the SF.gov page; the tenant may be entitled to a refund and may file a Tenant Petition for Unlawful Rent Increase for an Administrative Law Judge hearing.rule checked
Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)Sec. 37.15 Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S53)Report an unlawful rent increase (S25)Rent Board Forms Center (S26)
Wording differs between SF.gov pages - see flag F25.
Does the license requirement cover every kind of increase?
PRC-07
It covers annual allowable and banked increases on rent-controlled units. SF.gov states it is not required for Section 6.14/Costa-Hawkins increases or for passthroughs allowed under the Rent Ordinance.rule checked
Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)Rent Increases Under Section 6.14 And Costa-Hawkins (S16)Landlord Petitions and Passthroughs (S09)
Those increases have their own notice and petition rules.
Is the Housing Inventory information public?
PRC-08
Yes. SF.gov states the information entered in the Housing Inventory submission is public, and may include name and business, contact information, approximate square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms and vacancy/occupancy history.rule checked
The Rent Board Housing Inventory (S07)Sec. 37.15 Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S53)
Privacy note: the required filing publishes the owner's name and business contact information.
Does the license requirement apply if the unit is not rent-controlled?
PRC-09
No. SF.gov states the Rent Increase License rules apply only to units under San Francisco rent control.rule checked
Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)Learn about San Francisco rental laws (S06)

Step 1 - Confirm the unit is under the Rent Ordinance
STP-01
Many units built on or before June 13, 1979 that are not single-family dwellings have rent control; check the address in the SF Planning Property Information Map. Partial exemptions exist.rule checked
Learn about San Francisco rental laws (S06)San Francisco Property Information Map (S32)Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)
Exact address not supplied in this case.
Step 2 - Create a Rent Board Portal account
STP-02
Owners create an account in the Rent Board Portal to reach the Housing Inventory and the license record; paper filings can be emailed to the Rent Board or dropped off at its office.rule checked
Rent Board Portal (S55)The Rent Board Housing Inventory (S07)Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)
Office open 9-4 weekdays; counseling 415-252-4600.
Step 3 - Report each unit into the Housing Inventory
STP-03
Report by March 1 each year (buildings under 10 units since March 1, 2023) under penalty of perjury: owner or manager contact, square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy date and base rent in $250 increments.rule checked
The Rent Board Housing Inventory (S07)Sec. 37.15 Reporting Obligations; Licensing (S53)
Reporting is free; no monetary penalty is assessed for late filing.
Step 4 - Confirm a current license for each unit
STP-04
The license is generated for tenant-occupied units after reporting and must be current on the effective date of the increase, not merely later.rule checked
The Rent Board Housing Inventory (S07)Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)Rent Board Portal (S55)
Check status by address or APN in the portal.
Step 5 - Fix the anniversary date
STP-05
For an eligible periodic tenancy, the first annual increase may take effect 12 months after the tenancy began; after an increase, the increase date becomes the anniversary date.rule checked
Learn about rent increases in San Francisco (S01)California Tenants guide (Rev. 2022), fixed-term and periodic tenancies (S62)
Fixed-term leases need an express increase clause; actual lease type is unverified (F36). F36
Step 6 - Use the rate in effect on the effective date
STP-06
1.6% for increases effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027; the rate is 60% of the Bay Area CPI-U increase and Section 37.3 caps it at 7%.rule checked
Learn about rent increases in San Francisco (S01)Current Rates (S02)Form 571 Allowable Annual Increases 26-27 (S04)Sec. 37.3 Rent Limitations (S21)
No rate published yet for increases on or after 3/1/27 - flag F27.
Step 7 - Decide whether a banked increase applies
STP-07
A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase; banked percentages are added together, never compounded or prorated, never carry to a new tenant, and need no Rent Board petition.rule checked
Banked rent increases (S05)Annual rent increase for multiple years (S54)
SF.gov text/examples conflict on 12 vs 24 months; verify history and obtain counselor/legal review before relying on banking (F03). F03
Step 8 - Calculate on base rent only and do not round up
STP-08
Multiply base rent (including parking or storage charges) by the total percentage; temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges are excluded, and the result is not rounded up.rule checked
Learn about rent increases in San Francisco (S01)Annual Rent Increase 3/1/26-2/28/27 Announced (S03)Annual rent increase for multiple years (S54)
See the Calculations page for the illustrative arithmetic.
Step 9 - File any petition or worksheet before the notice
STP-09
File required petitions before notice; file utility worksheets before notice; bond/water passthroughs use their specific worksheet rules.rule checked
Landlord Petitions and Passthroughs (S09)Special Circumstances Petitions Based on Rents for Comparable Units (S10)Operating and Maintenance Expense Petitions (S11)Capital Improvement Petitions (S12)Utility Passthroughs (S13)Tenant In Occupancy Petitions (S14)
A plain annual or banked increase needs no petition. Do not treat every passthrough as petition-based.
Step 10 - Serve written notice with the required contents
STP-10
At least 30 days' written notice, or 90 days if the increase by itself or combined with another increase in the same year is more than 10%; add 5 days if mailed. State the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date and any banked portion.rule checked
Learn about rent increases in San Francisco (S01)Banked rent increases (S05)CIV 827 (S35)
Civil Code 827 requires longer notice in some cases.
Step 11 - Deliver it properly and keep proof
STP-11
Personal delivery or mail under Civil Code 827 and Code of Civil Procedure 1013; keep the notice, proof of service and the license record with the unit file.rule checked
CIV 827 (S35)Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)Rent Board Portal (S55)
The license need not be attached to the notice.
Step 12 - Expect tenant-side review and apply the same rule to each unit
STP-12
Tenant petitions are free and have no filing deadline for an unlawful increase; a tenant may search the portal for license status. Apply the same written, neutral, dated process to every eligible unit.rule checked
Report an unlawful rent increase (S25)Rent Board Forms Center (S26)Notice of Owner Noncompliance with Housing Inventory (S08)24 CFR 100.500 Discriminatory effect (S42)
No rule requires raising both units - see Analysis.
