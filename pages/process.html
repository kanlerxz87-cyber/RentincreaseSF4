Must an individual property owner (not a corporation) get a Rent Increase License before raising rent?
PRC-01		Yes. A landlord must have a current Rent Increase License on file before any annual or banked increase can take effect. The requirement follows the unit's rent-controlled status, not the owner's size or corporate form.verifiedS01 S07 S08 S53No owner-type, individual-owner or small-portfolio exemption appears on the official pages.
What does the Rent Ordinance itself say?
PRC-02		Section 37.3(a)(1): a landlord who has reported the required information under Section 37.15 shall have a license to impose an annual increase not exceeding 60% of the published CPI increase, never more than 7%. Section 37.15(e): a substantially compliant owner receives the license; otherwise it is suspended and the tenant is not obligated to pay the increased rent for the months of the suspension.verifiedS21 S53The license condition is written into the ordinance, not only the SF.gov summaries.
Is there a separate licence application form I have to file?
PRC-20		No. The Rent Board describes the licence as generated by the Housing Inventory report: 'once you have reported into the Housing Inventory, a rent increase license will be automatically generated for non-owner occupied units.' SF.gov's increase page states the same route: 'To get a rent increase license, a landlord must first report into the Rent Board Housing Inventory.' There is no separate licence application and no separate licence fee - SF.gov states that reporting into the Housing Inventory 'is free and can be done online 24/7' (the annual Rent Board Fee is a different obligation).verifiedS07 S01The Rent Board Fee (S56) IS a separate annual payment and has its own exemption request window - do not confuse the two (flag F33).
So "informing the Rent Board" and "applying for the licence" are the same step?
PRC-21		Yes - both refer to the Housing Inventory report. Reporting is done through the Rent Board Portal, is free, is available 24/7 online, is due March 1 every year, and is submitted under penalty of perjury.verifiedS07 S55 S53The report is the licence-generating act; the licence itself does not need to be attached to the tenant notice.
Is this a state requirement, a local requirement, or both?
PRC-22		Local only. It is San Francisco Administrative Code Sec. 37.15 (added by Ord. 265-20, effective January 18, 2021). No California statute requires reporting a rent increase to a rent board; the 2023 state law (SB 567) addressed just cause and the state rent cap and became operative April 1, 2024.verifiedS53 S90 S88 S73Full layer table on the Local Law page: LAW-01 to LAW-08, summarized on the Executive Summary at SL-01 to SL-05. Flag F53.
Is there a small-owner or fewer-than-10-unit exemption?
PRC-03		No exemption found. Condominiums and units in buildings with fewer than 10 residential units have been required to report since March 1, 2023, with updates every March 1.verifiedS07 S08 S53Applies to this two-unit building if the units are rent-controlled.
Must the owner report into the Housing Inventory first?
PRC-04		Yes. All owners of residential property are required to report; a rent increase license is generated for units reported as tenant-occupied.verifiedS07 S53Free; annual deadline March 1; no monetary penalty for late filing.
Does informing the Rent Board mean filing each increase notice?
PRC-05		The official pages describe the license as arising from the annual Housing Inventory report. No separate filing of an individual increase notice is described, and the license does not need to be included with the notice.verifiedS07 S53 S01Read as absence of a stated requirement; confirm with a Rent Board counselor.
What happens if a notice goes into effect without a current license?
PRC-06		The notice may be null and void (possibly for multiple years) or unenforceable, depending on the SF.gov page; the tenant may be entitled to a refund and may file a Tenant Petition for Unlawful Rent Increase for an Administrative Law Judge hearing.verifiedS08 S53 S25 S26Wording differs between SF.gov pages - see flag F25.
Does the license requirement cover every kind of increase?
PRC-07		It covers annual allowable and banked increases on rent-controlled units. SF.gov states it is not required for Section 6.14/Costa-Hawkins increases or for passthroughs allowed under the Rent Ordinance.verifiedS08 S16 S09Those increases have their own notice and petition rules.
Is the Housing Inventory information public?
PRC-08		Yes. SF.gov states the information entered in the Housing Inventory submission is public, and may include name and business, contact information, approximate square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms and vacancy/occupancy history.verifiedS07 S53Privacy note: the required filing publishes the owner's name and business contact information.
Does the license requirement apply if the unit is not rent-controlled?
PRC-09		No. SF.gov states the Rent Increase License rules apply only to units under San Francisco rent control.verifiedS08 S06Coverage of the specific address is unverified in this case.

Step-by-step process (with official links)

Step 1 - Confirm the unit is under the Rent Ordinance
STP-01		Many units built on or before June 13, 1979 that are not single-family dwellings have rent control; check the address in the SF Planning Property Information Map. Partial exemptions exist.verifiedS06 S32 S08Exact address not supplied in this case.
Step 2 - Create a Rent Board Portal account
STP-02		Owners create an account in the Rent Board Portal to reach the Housing Inventory and the license record; paper filings can be emailed to the Rent Board or dropped off at its office.verifiedS55 S07 S08Office open 9-4 weekdays; counseling 415-252-4600.
Step 3 - Report each unit into the Housing Inventory
STP-03		Report by March 1 each year (buildings under 10 units since March 1, 2023) under penalty of perjury: owner or manager contact, square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, occupancy date and base rent in $250 increments.verifiedS07 S53Reporting is free; no monetary penalty is assessed for late filing.
Step 4 - Confirm a current license for each unit
STP-04		The license is generated for tenant-occupied units after reporting and must be current on the effective date of the increase, not merely later.verifiedS07 S08 S55Check status by address or APN in the portal.
Step 5 - Fix the anniversary date
STP-05		The first annual increase may be imposed 12 months after the lease began; after an increase, the increase date becomes the anniversary date and the next increase needs at least 12 more months.verifiedS01A later increase date becomes the new anniversary date.
Step 6 - Use the rate in effect on the effective date
STP-06		1.6% for increases effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027; the rate is 60% of the Bay Area CPI-U increase and Section 37.3 caps it at 7%.verifiedS01 S02 S04 S21No rate published yet for increases on or after 3/1/27 - flag F27.
Step 7 - Decide whether a banked increase applies
STP-07		A skipped increase is only banked if at least 24 full months pass without any rent increase; banked percentages are added together, never compounded or prorated, never carry to a new tenant, and need no Rent Board petition.verifiedS05 S54SF.gov examples describe elapsed time differently - flag F03.
Step 8 - Calculate on base rent only and do not round up
STP-08		Multiply base rent (including parking or storage charges) by the total percentage; temporary passthroughs and fluctuating charges are excluded, and the result is not rounded up.verifiedS01 S03 S54See the Calculations page for the illustrative arithmetic.
Step 9 - File any petition or worksheet before the notice
STP-09		Capital improvement, operating and maintenance, utility, comparable-rent and bond/water passthrough increases each have their own petition or worksheet rules that apply before the increase is noticed.verifiedS09 S10 S11 S12 S13 S14Not needed for a plain annual or banked increase.
Step 10 - Serve written notice with the required contents
STP-10		At least 30 days' written notice, or 90 days if the increase by itself or combined with another increase in the same year is more than 10%; add 5 days if mailed. State the dollar amount, the percentage, the effective date and any banked portion.verifiedS01 S05 S35Civil Code 827 requires longer notice in some cases.
Step 11 - Deliver it properly and keep proof
STP-11		Personal delivery or mail under Civil Code 827 and Code of Civil Procedure 1013; keep the notice, proof of service and the license record with the unit file.verifiedS35 S08 S55The license need not be attached to the notice.
Step 12 - Expect tenant-side review and apply the same rule to each unit
STP-12		Tenant petitions are free and have no filing deadline for an unlawful increase; a tenant may search the portal for license status. Apply the same written, neutral, dated process to every eligible unit.verifiedS25 S26 S08 S42No rule requires raising both units - see Analysis.
